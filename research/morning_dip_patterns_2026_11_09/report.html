Feature iteration · exact candidate definitions

Keep the dip measurements and test their interactions

A feature can have a weak average return on its own and still help the model when combined with trend, volume, volatility, news or other inputs. The descriptive cohort study below did not test that incremental contribution. It therefore does not settle whether these features should be added.

For each setup, keep both continuous measurements and a deterministic flag. Continuous values preserve the size of the effect; flags make a specific pattern reproducible and inspectable. The following cutoffs are initial hypotheses, not validated buying instructions. Existing features remain the baseline throughout.

How to assess predictiveness: compare the current Rank/Fit model with and without each new family on identical later-date folds, then check returns after costs, downside and stability. Threshold refinement belongs inside training dates. A rigid definition becomes a useful predictive trigger only after it shows added value on later observations.

Descriptive baseline · not an incremental feature test

What the simple conditions do on their own

“Two down days” means both previous close-to-close returns were at most −2%. A 52-week low means the lowest low over the 252 completed sessions before entry. “Near” means yesterday closed within 2% of that low. We evaluated every eligible occurrence, including stocks never traded, with no selection from our current Rank list.

Motivating example · full eligible history included in the screen

MRCY illustrates the state we want to measure

November 4 and 5 lost -10.66% and -8.65% close-to-close, a combined -18.39%. The November 5 low of $64.695 broke the prior 252-session low of $65.04 from November 21, 2025.

On November 6 it opened at $66.93, a +2.11% gap. That had recovered only 9.34% of the two-day closing loss, so “still deeply depressed” describes the setup better than “no bounce yet.” It closed at $73.55: a hypothetical open-to-close gain of +9.89%.

It first fell another 3.82% below that open, reaching $64.3706 in the minute beginning 09:36 ET. That later low is an outcome, not information available for an opening decision. The favorable full-session return did not imply a smooth path or identify the exact bottom.

Four candidate families · not added to live models

Measure the state of the dip

The saved Rank model already serves 132 inputs; Fit serves 139. Both include the 19 recent price-pattern inputs and 57 open-pattern seasonalities. Returns, candle shapes, volume and volatility already cover much of this territory. Raw streak, short RSI and several dip-story features have previously been pruned. Simply adding more familiar indicators would duplicate existing information.

Candidate 1

Long-term low location and fresh break

Is the entry near a previously observed floor, or below it?

low252 = min(L[t-252:t]); distance = O[t] / low252 - 1; breach = O[t] < low252; low_age = sessions since the most recent occurrence of low252.

Available: Prior low and its age are pre-open; distance and breach require the observed opening price.

What is new: The current pipeline has short-range patterns and trailing-peak drawdowns, but no genuine 252-session low context.

Do not use today's eventual low. Require 252 completed valid sessions and distinguish missing history from a neutral value.

Candidate 2

Selloff severity and recovery already consumed

How unusually large was the decline, and how much has recovered before entry?

two_day_return = C[t-1] / C[t-3] - 1; severity = two_day_return / (std20(returns ending t-3) * sqrt(2)); close_loss_recovered = (O[t] - C[t-1]) / (C[t-3] - C[t-1]), defined only for a positive prior closing loss.

Available: Severity is pre-open; recovery fraction requires the observed open.

What is new: The two-day return already exists. The proposed addition is recovery fraction and its interaction with long-term low context, using volatility measured before the shock.

Negative fractions mean continued decline; values above one mean more than the closing loss recovered. Do not clip these cases together. A separate low-based recovery fraction uses a different denominator and must have a different name.

Candidate 3

Selling-pressure change during the decline

Was the second down day less forceful, and did buyers lift the price away from its low?

Compare absolute prior daily losses, true ranges and dollar volumes between t-2 and t-1, scaled by baselines ending t-3; retain yesterday's close location (C-L)/(H-L) and lower-wick fraction as interaction inputs.

Available: All inputs stop at yesterday's close.

What is new: Volume, candle shapes and volatility already exist. Test incremental interactions conditional on selloff/low location rather than add duplicate indicators.

High volume can reflect new information as well as temporary selling. No current-day full-session volume. Zero-range bars and missing denominators need explicit handling.

Candidate 4

Current gap and previous selling volume

Does the market open stronger after heavy selling, or continue to break down?

gap[t] = O[t] / C[t-1] - 1; gap_previous_volume = gap[t] * volume_zscore[t-1], optionally interacted with prior selloff severity.

Available: Open-known; must be calculated identically in historical rows and live inference.

What is new: Existing gap_volume_interaction uses same-day completed volume and is shifted as a whole. It therefore represents yesterday's gap times yesterday's volume, not this interaction.

An opening gap does not establish that a bottom has formed. Observing the open does not guarantee an execution at that exact price.

My recommended first slice: low distance / fresh-break state plus recovery fraction. They address the missing 252-session context and the user's “hasn't recovered yet” idea directly. The volume and pressure interactions come next, subject to incremental testing. No direction is hard-coded as “good”: the model should be allowed to learn that some apparently cheap setups are unfavorable.

Keep prior overnight losses separate from prior regular-session losses. Later research can also test stock-specific selling relative to SPY and timestamped news. A 09:35/09:45 reclaim or stabilization signal is useful to investigate separately, with a later executable entry and the same day's close as exit.

Audit and source locations: feature_audit.json. Existing gap×volume timing: features/candle.py:17; shift contract: training/dataset.py:44. These four full interactions have not been fit or tested for incremental model value in this cohort screen.

Primary literature · horizons matter

Reversal has mechanisms, but it can happen before we buy

The strongest caution comes from Lou, Polk and Skouras: their conventional short-term reversal portfolio earns its positive component overnight and has a negative intraday component. Their monthly sorting horizon is different from our two-day rule, but it demonstrates why we must test the exact trading session.

Primary studySample and horizonEvidence and limits
A tug of war: Overnight versus intraday expected returns (2019)Core US CRSP/Compustat/TAQ sample 1993–2013; robustness includes nine large non-US equity markets.

Monthly sorting signals and subsequent monthly aggregates of daily overnight and intraday returns. Main open proxy is 9:30–10:00 VWAP, with robustness to actual opening prices and bid-ask midpoint.		Past overnight returns predict subsequent overnight continuation and intraday reversal; past intraday returns predict subsequent intraday continuation and overnight reversal. The conventional short-term reversal premium and four momentum-strategy premia accrue overnight in their decomposition; close-to-close success cannot be assumed to survive an open-to-close strategy.

This is not a test of two down days followed by one open-to-close trade. Published long-short gross portfolio alphas do not establish the user’s executable long-only net edge; 30-minute VWAP is not the opening auction.
Decomposing Short-Term Return Reversal (2011)January 1982–March 2009; non-penny stocks with sufficient analyst forecasts, relatively large/liquid and about 75% of US equity capitalization.

Previous-month signals and following-month returns; original analysis uses I/B/E/S-month timing, with calendar-month robustness.		Within-industry residual price changes unexplained by cash-flow news drive reversal; industry momentum and underreaction to cash-flow news offset indiscriminate reversal. Buying residual losers is associated with liquidity shocks; selling residual winners with sentiment. The authors report midpoint-return robustness and explicitly estimate trading costs.

Their residual is not simply stock minus sector: expected returns and analyst forecast revisions also enter. Monthly forecast revisions and monthly holding evidence do not validate a next-morning intraday rule.
Evaporating Liquidity (2011)US NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq CRSP stocks, January 1998–December 2010.

Daily close-to-close reversal portfolios averaging five sub-strategies using lagged returns from t−1 through t−5.		Expected short-term reversal returns rise with VIX, consistent with compensation for supplying liquidity when intermediaries withdraw. Both transaction-price and quote-midpoint returns are studied; quote data are available for Nasdaq stocks.

Transaction-price returns model a hypothetical liquidity supplier executing at the closing print; a market-order user pays execution costs. This is close-to-close evidence, not a demonstrated next-open-to-close edge.
The 52-Week High and Momentum Investing (2004)CRSP stocks, July 1963–December 2001.

Portfolios formed monthly using price relative to the preceding 12-month high; held six months. Regression tests skip one month to reduce bid-ask effects.		Nearness to a 52-week high predicts stronger subsequent returns and accounts for much conventional momentum. Their anchoring interpretation allows bad-news stocks far below prior highs to continue falling.

This is six-month momentum evidence, not evidence that buying today’s high beats today’s dip. Far below a 52-week high is not equivalent to hitting a fresh 52-week low.
Trading Volume and Serial Correlation in Stock Returns (1992)Main CRSP NYSE/AMEX value-weighted index sample July 3, 1962–September 30, 1987; robustness through December 30, 1988, earlier index data, DJIA and 32 large stocks.

Daily returns; regressions of the next day’s return on lagged return interacted with detrended volume. No overnight/session separation.		Return autocorrelation declines as volume rises; in the model, high-volume declines are more consistent with noninformational selling pressure and subsequently higher expected returns.

Primarily aggregate-market evidence from an older market structure, not a modern stock-specific intraday entry test. Volume can also accompany information events; this source does not justify treating every high-volume selloff as capitulation.

Two additional papers were checked at publisher-abstract level only: Berkman et al. (2012) on retail attention, expensive opening prices and intraday reversal; and Heston et al. (2010) on half-hour return patterns and very short liquidity reversals. They motivate timing research; their unverified sample details and strategy magnitudes are not used here.

Five full-text primary papers had relevant sections inspected. How to interpret this screen

Timing, controls and limitations

  1. Fixed cohort definitions; transparent inclusion correction. Seventeen cohorts, 1%/2%/3% shock thresholds, costs and temporal diagnostics were fixed before the original outcome calculation. After that screen, the user directed inclusion of MRCY; this version changes that membership only. No threshold optimization followed. The 1% and 3% rows are sensitivity checks. Cohorts overlap and are not independent strategies. The new precise feature-trigger proposals above have not been fitted or validated by this descriptive screen.
  2. Morning information only. Historical inputs stop at yesterday; open-conditioned rules also use the observed opening price. The label is C[t]/O[t]−1. The 252-session warmup reduces the five-year raw cache to November 1, 2022–November 6, 2026 for evaluated rows. MRCY is included; SPY and the frozen 200-name production holdout are excluded.
  3. Eligibility and controls. Open at least $5, previous 20-session average dollar volume at least $10m, and 252 valid completed prior bars. No future 21-day completeness filter or cooldown. Each event is compared with nonmembers in the same date/volatility quintile, with at least five controls. Volatility is measured over 20 returns ending before the two-day shock. All reported main groups have full matched-row coverage.
  4. Retrospective evidence. The hypotheses were motivated by a known recent example, and this history was used in earlier research. The 2025/2026 slices are temporal diagnostics, not untouched validation. The underlying registry is today's universe, excluding many dead/delisted historical names; 17 share-class symbols also lack cache coverage. No claim of a complete historical investable universe is made.
  5. Price basis and events. The cached daily bars are split-adjusted, exclude cash dividends and lack full five-year corporate-action certification. The historical $5 filter is on retrospectively adjusted prices. Ex-dividend dates, spinoffs and information-driven declines can mimic dips. This screen does not reproduce the live earnings/news eligibility filter, match on sector, or estimate a causal treatment effect.
  6. Execution remains unvalidated. The official open and close are benchmarks. Nasdaq's opening cross occurs at 09:30 ET; individual NYSE securities may open later. Inference: observing the final opening print cannot guarantee execution at that already established price. Open-conditioned candidates need a later-entry test, with spreads, slippage, halts and individual opening times.
  7. Costs and tail risk. The 0/10/25/50 bps scenarios subtract a constant total round-trip cost. They do not model size, actual spread, fees, impact or a stop. Internally valid extreme returns remain included; 1% tail clipping is shown separately. Uncertainty intervals account for weekly clustering but not all persistence, data bias or multiple testing.

The current production download is approximately one year. A genuine 252-session-low feature needs longer ingestion and warmup before there is adequate training coverage. Research-cache results must not be silently mixed with the production raw-price basis.

Primary protocol and audit trail: protocol.json · frozen membership and source hashes · quality and extreme returns · run manifest · per-date returns · independent numerical verification.
